Chapter 8: Digital Society and Cyber Security

8.1 Digital Society and Computer Ethics

A digital society is a society where computers, the Internet and other digital technologies are widely used in daily activities. Computer ethics refers to the moral principles that guide the responsible and proper use of computers. Some common ethical issues in computing are:

  • Privacy violation
  • Software piracy
  • Plagiarism
  • Unauthorized access
  • Misuse of information

Computer crimes include hacking, data theft, identity theft and online fraud. Strong passwords, antivirus software, firewalls and regular updates help improve computer security.

8.2 Concept of Information Security

Information security is the process of protecting information from unauthorized access, use, modification, disclosure or destruction. Common threats to information security include:

  • Hacking and unauthorized access
  • Malware and viruses
  • Phishing attacks
  • Weak passwords
  • Data theft and system failure

Encryption and Decryption

Encryption is the process of converting readable information into an unreadable form called ciphertext to prevent unauthorized access. Decryption converts the encrypted information back into its original readable form using an appropriate key. These techniques are commonly used to protect sensitive data and online communication.

8.3 Concept of Cybercrime

Cybercrime refers to illegal activities carried out using computers, mobile devices, networks or the Internet. Common types of cybercrime include hacking, phishing, identity theft, cyberbullying, online fraud, data theft and unauthorized access.

Cyber Laws and Their Implications

Cyber laws are rules and regulations made to control activities in cyberspace and punish cyber offences. They provide legal protection for users, electronic transactions and digital information. Cybercrime can cause financial loss, privacy violations, loss of data and damage to the reputation of individuals or organizations.

8.4 Malicious Software and Spam

Malicious software, commonly known as malware, is software designed to damage computer systems, steal information or perform unauthorized activities. The major types of malware are:

  • Virus: A program that attaches itself to files and can spread when the file is used.
  • Worm: A harmful program that can spread automatically through networks.
  • Trojan Horse: A program that appears useful but performs harmful activities.
  • Spyware: Software that secretly collects information about users.
  • Ransomware: Malware that restricts access to data and demands payment.

Phishing is a technique used to trick users into providing sensitive information through fake emails, messages or websites. Spam refers to unwanted electronic messages, usually sent in large numbers. Users should avoid suspicious links and attachments and should not provide personal information to unknown sources.

8.5 Protection from Cybercrime

Cybersecurity measures help protect computers, networks and digital information from cyber threats. Important security measures include:

  • Using strong and unique passwords
  • Enabling two-factor authentication
  • Keeping software and operating systems updated
  • Avoiding suspicious links, websites and attachments
  • Using trusted antivirus software
  • Taking regular backups of important data

Firewall

A firewall is a security system that monitors and controls incoming and outgoing network traffic. It helps prevent unauthorized access to a computer or network.

Antivirus Software

Antivirus software is used to detect, prevent and remove viruses and other harmful programs from a computer. It should be regularly updated to provide better protection against new threats.

Data Backup

Data backup is the process of creating an additional copy of important information. It helps recover data in case of accidental deletion, hardware failure, malware attacks or other unexpected problems.

8.6 Intellectual Property Right

Intellectual Property Right (IPR) refers to the legal rights given to creators and inventors for their original works and inventions. The major forms of IPR are:

  • Copyright: Protects creative works such as books, music, videos and software.
  • Patent: Protects new and useful inventions.
  • Trademark: Protects names, logos, symbols and designs used to identify products or services.

IPR is important in digital content because it prevents unauthorized copying, distribution and use of creative works. It also encourages creativity, innovation and the development of new ideas.

8.7 Concept of Digital Signature

A digital signature is an electronic method used to verify the identity of the sender and the authenticity of a digital document or message. It also ensures that the information has not been altered after it was signed. Digital signatures are commonly used in electronic documents, online transactions and other digital services.

8.8 Concept of Cyber Law in Nepal

Cyber law in Nepal provides legal provisions for regulating computers, electronic communication and Internet activities. The Electronic Transactions Act, 2063 (2006) is an important law related to electronic transactions, digital signatures and computer-related offences. It also provides legal recognition to electronic records and helps control the misuse of digital technology.

8.9 ICT Policy in Nepal

ICT policy in Nepal focuses on the development and effective use of Information and Communication Technology for national development. It aims to expand digital infrastructure, improve digital literacy and develop ICT skills. It also promotes e-governance, digital services, innovation and the secure use of technology.

ICT is widely used in education, health, banking, business and government services. The proper development of ICT helps improve communication, access to information and the delivery of services.
