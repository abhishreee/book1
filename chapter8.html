Chapter 8: Digital Society and Cyber Security

8.1 Digital Society and Computer Ethics

A digital society is a society where computers, the Internet and digital technologies are widely used in everyday life. Computer ethics refers to the moral principles and rules that guide the responsible use of computers and technology. Ethical issues in computing include privacy violation, software piracy, plagiarism, unauthorized access and misuse of information. Computer crimes include hacking, identity theft, data theft and online fraud. Such crimes can be reduced by using strong passwords, antivirus software, firewalls, regular updates and responsible online practices.

8.2 Concept of Information Security

Information security is the process of protecting information from unauthorized access, use, modification, disclosure or destruction. Threats to information security include hacking, malware, phishing, weak passwords, data theft and system failures. Encryption converts readable information into an unreadable form called ciphertext so that unauthorized users cannot understand it. Decryption converts the encrypted information back into its original form using an appropriate key. Encryption and decryption are widely used to protect personal information and online communication.

8.3 Concept of Cybercrime

Cybercrime refers to illegal activities performed using computers, mobile devices, networks or the Internet. Common types of cybercrime include hacking, phishing, identity theft, cyberbullying, online fraud, data theft and unauthorized access to systems. Cyber laws are laws and regulations created to control such activities and protect people and organizations in cyberspace. Cybercrime may result in financial loss, privacy violations, data loss and damage to the reputation of individuals or organizations.

8.4 Malicious Software and Spam

Malicious software, commonly called malware, is software designed to damage a computer, steal information or perform unauthorized activities. Its major types include viruses, worms, Trojan horses, spyware and ransomware. Phishing is a technique in which attackers use fake messages, websites or emails to trick users into revealing sensitive information. Spam refers to unwanted messages sent in large numbers, often through email or messaging services. These threats can be prevented by avoiding suspicious links and attachments, using updated antivirus software and keeping operating systems and applications updated.

8.5 Protection from Cybercrime

Cybersecurity involves protecting computers, networks and digital information from cyber threats. Important cybersecurity measures include using strong passwords, enabling two-factor authentication, updating software and avoiding suspicious websites and files. A firewall monitors and controls network traffic and helps prevent unauthorized access. Antivirus software detects and removes malware from computer systems. Data backup means keeping an additional copy of important data, which helps recover information in case of accidental deletion, system failure or cyberattack.

8.6 Intellectual Property Right

Intellectual Property Right (IPR) refers to the legal rights given to creators and inventors for their original works and inventions. Copyright protects creative works such as books, music, photographs, videos and software. Patents provide legal protection for new and useful inventions, while trademarks protect names, logos, symbols and designs that identify products or services. IPR is important in digital content because it prevents unauthorized copying, distribution and use of creative works and encourages creativity and innovation.

8.7 Concept of Digital Signature

A digital signature is an electronic method used to verify the identity of the sender and the authenticity of a digital document or message. It also helps ensure that the information has not been changed after it was signed. Digital signatures provide authenticity, integrity and security to electronic communication. They are commonly used in electronic documents, online transactions and digital services.

8.8 Concept of Cyber Law in Nepal

Cyber law in Nepal provides legal provisions for regulating computers, electronic communication, digital transactions and Internet activities. The Electronic Transactions Act, 2063 (2006) is an important law related to electronic transactions, digital signatures and computer-related offences. It provides legal recognition to electronic records and helps control activities such as unauthorized access, electronic fraud and misuse of digital information. Cyber law helps create a safer environment for the use of digital technology in Nepal.

8.9 ICT Policy in Nepal

ICT policy in Nepal focuses on the development and effective use of Information and Communication Technology for national development. It aims to expand digital infrastructure, improve Internet and communication services, increase digital literacy and develop ICT skills. ICT is used in education, health, banking, business and government services. The policy also promotes e-governance, digital services, innovation and the secure use of information technology to build a digital and knowledge-based society.
